# Staging and composition

Read this before placing anything in a frame. Tags: [E] published standard, [C] common practice, [H] house rule learnt by
looking at many results (tune it, do not worship it).

## 1. Say the idea first
Write one sentence: "After this shot the viewer knows that ___." If the sentence has an "and", you have two shots.
Everything in the frame either serves that sentence or is removed. [H]

## 2. Subject size
The most common failure is a correct object that is too small to read. Measure it, do not eyeball it.

| Situation | Subject should take | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Establishing shot (where are we) | 35-55 % of frame height (group of things) | enough room to show the setting, still recognisable [H] |
| Medium (one person or one object with its support) | 50-70 % of frame height | the action is legible [H] |
| Close-up of a face or object | 55-70 % of frame width | detail readable on a phone [H] |
| Detail insert (a hand, a rim, a valve) | about 60 % of frame width | one thing only [H] |
| Interactive 3D lesson, first screen | model covers at least 6 % of the *stage* area at rest, aim for 25-45 % | below 6 % the model reads as a speck in a box [H] |
| Picture beside text in a book page | about 60 % of the picture area's height, 70 % of its width | thin pictures waste the page [H] |

Worked example (portrait video, 1080 x 1920, a beating heart): at 35 % frame height the heart is 670 px tall, chambers
about 200 px: labels fit. At 12 % it is 230 px tall: chambers 60 px, labels would have to be tiny. Re-frame, do not
shrink the labels.

Subjects measured from the *bounding box of the thing itself*, not of the whole scene including guide lines, floors,
halos and plates. A camera fitted to a bounding sphere with a 22 % margin that includes helpers leaves the real object
under 6 % of its stage. Fit to the visible object, then add margin. [H]

## 3. One idea per shot, one shot per sentence
- A shot lasts 2.2-5.5 s (see `camera.md`). Within it, one new thing appears, or one thing changes. [H]
- Vary the size from one sentence to the next (wide, medium, close). Start a new thing with a wider shot, then go close. [H]
- Show the whole thing first so it is recognised, then cut it open or zoom. A viewer who never saw the closed heart
  cannot place the open one.

## 4. Object before controls
For anything interactive: the first screen shows the thing itself, large, before sliders, formulas and menus. Fold
secondary controls behind one button. Measure at a 1366 x 768 viewport: the stage should reach the bottom of the first
screen, at least 90 % of the stage must be visible without scrolling, and the stage should take at least 40 % of the
viewport. A title row of 3 rem plus a sentence plus a toolbar can push the stage 300-440 px down; that is the fault
to look for. [H]

## 5. Where things sit
- Keep the subject in the band between top overlays and the subtitle zone (bands per format in `camera.md` section 5). In
  portrait, a subject centre about 44 % from the top works when there is a tracker or title above and subtitles below. [H]
- Leave room below a close detail for captions: the thing sits in the upper two thirds. [H]
- Never let a label, ring, arrow or shadow cross a face or the key detail. A decorative ring crossing a head in a wide
  shot was caught only by looking at the frame at full size.
- A tag near a frame edge opens toward the middle, or its word runs out of the frame. [H]
- A very large copy of a small thing needs a backdrop (wall, cloth, water). Against the open sky of the set it floats and
  loses scale. [H]
- Things on a worktop are hidden by an over-the-shoulder camera and a head ends up in a corner. For small things use a
  close shot from the side and above (azimuth 70-80 degrees, elevation 35-40). [H]
- Never film people from behind their heads when the story is about their faces or hands. Look back at them from the
  front quarter. [H]
- A top view of a person shows the head: draw arcs above head height or the head hides them. [H]
- Give a moving thing a still line to be read against (a dashed level line, the road edge). Motion is only visible
  relative to something that does not move. [H]

## 6. Fill, density, empty space
- A wide shot whose subject is under about 20 % of the frame height, with a flat backdrop filling most of the rest, reads as
  "nothing happening". Cut closer (to the 35 % of the establishing row above) or add context
  that is true to the scene. [H]
- Do not fill space with decoration. Space around the subject is fine; space *used by a half-empty grid* is not.
- A loop that empties and restarts can be caught empty by a still capture. Check stills at several times. [H]

## 7. Things that need their own light
Objects on a screen, lamps, glowing organs, "hot" parts: give them a little emission of their own. If only the room
light decides how they look, they vanish in a dim scene. [H]

## 8. Faults seen repeatedly (look for these first)
head in a corner; subject under 6 % of its stage; correct picture but the text's example is not the one drawn; label
sitting on a line it should sit beside; two labels printed on top of each other; a stray object inside a block; a ring
crossing a head; an empty backdrop above a tiny subject; a hand covering the part being explained.

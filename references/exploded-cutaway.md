# Exploded and cut-open views

Use when the lesson is about what is inside or how parts fit: an engine, a heart, a phone, a tooth, a pulley block.
Tags: [C] common, [H] house rule.

## 1. Choosing
| Need | View |
|---|---|
| order of assembly, which part touches which | exploded view |
| inside of a hollow thing, flow through it | cut-open (section) view |
| layers of a thin thing (skin, a phone screen) | layer split along one axis |
| something too small to see | very large copy of the small thing (a "macro" stage) with a backdrop |

Always show the whole, closed object first for 1-2 seconds so the viewer recognises it. Then cut or explode. [H]

## 2. Building parts that explode cleanly
- Draw the components the real object has, at its real proportions, and skip ornament that stands for nothing: it teaches
  nothing and hides the parts that matter. [C]
- Prefer simple closed shapes for parts; where two overlap, shift one or split it. [C]
- Group parts into assemblies that move together; give each assembly a pivot or axis. [H]
- Printed markings, scales and engraving are flat decals on the surface, not extra geometry. [H]
- **Interpenetration check**: sample the explode every 5-10 % of its travel and test every pair of solids for overlap.
  Do this from a few directions, not only the front. Zero overlaps at the assembled pose is a baseline; overlaps during
  the move are acceptable only if the parts are visibly passing through a gap you drew. [H]

## 3. Explode motion
- Direction: one axis (the assembly axis) or radially, never both in one picture. [H]
- Order: peel from the outside in: outer parts depart first, inner ones follow 40-100 ms behind; reverse on the way back. [C]
- Distance: each part moves by about 0.5-1.5 times its own thickness along the axis; the total spread is no more than 2
  times the assembled size. More than that and the whole no longer reads as one thing. [H]
- Duration: 1.0-1.6 s with the glide curve; hold at the exploded pose for the reading time of whatever labels are
  shown, at least 1.5 s. [H]
- Leader lines from each label to its part stay attached while the part moves. [H]
- Keep the parts' relative order and spacing visible: thin dashed axis lines between parts help (colour the neutral). [H]

## 4. Cut-open views
- A clipping plane or a cut mesh: show the **cut face** in its own tone (lighter, or hatched) so the viewer sees where the
  slice passed through material. [H]
- Cut where the story is: through the chamber, the valve, the nozzle. Slicing through the centre of a symmetric object
  often hides the interesting asymmetry. [H]
- Outline both the outer surface and the cut edge; the outline weight of the cut edge is about 1.3 times the outer one. [H]
- The thing you are explaining is at least 50 % of the frame width in the cut. If the cut shrinks it, re-frame. [H]
- Nothing inside may be clipped by the cut plane in a way that shows an empty shell when it should show a solid. [H]
- Do not light one region inside unless the text names it. A glow over the whole organ is truthful; a spot is a claim.

## 5. Very large copies
- Show the real-size object, then the very large copy fading in with a connecting cue (a zoom ring, a thin line). [H]
- Give it a backdrop (a wall, cloth, water, a dark card) or it reads against the sky of the set. [H]
- Hide the very large copy in every shot that does not use it: from a far or high camera it shows up in the sky. [H]
- State the magnification when it matters: "about 1000 times bigger". [H]
- Keep line weights thicker in the copy than a naive scale gives, or the lines vanish. [H]

## 6. Flow inside a cut-open object
- Use beads or arrows in the flowing colour; the speed should differ where the physics differs (slower in the thicker
  place). [H]
- One flowing thing per picture; a second flowing thing gets its own colour and its own beat. [H]
- If the lesson is about *where* something is taken back or added, change the colour of the flow at that place. [H]

## 7. Faults seen
shapes that stand in for a real thing but are not recognisable at first sight (a red disc and a grey slab for a scanner);
white lumps in a white haze; the thing too small in a pale box; a cut view that hides the labelled part; the glass casing
vanishing against a pale ground (give it an outline and a slight tint); parts named "Part 1..13" (give readable names).

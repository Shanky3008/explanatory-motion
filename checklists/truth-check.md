# Truth check

One failed item blocks release. Do not average. Read the source text next to the picture, sentence by sentence.

## Source fidelity
- [ ] The example in the picture is the example in the text (same numbers, same objects).
- [ ] Every number, label and readout in the picture can be traced to the source or recomputed by code.
- [ ] Nothing is lettered or labelled that the source does not say.
- [ ] Each label points at its own part (read label and leader end together); left and right are the object's own sides.
- [ ] A reference picture you were given has been checked label by label against the source, not copied as true.
- [ ] Spelling and notation follow the source (one spelling per term; superscripts and subscripts correct to the end of
      the line; no code text in captions).

## Physical and logical truth
- [ ] Rays bend only at boundaries; reflections leave from the face that was hit; opaque things stop rays.
- [ ] Directions (flow, force, current, field) match the physics and the convention stated.
- [ ] Quantities drawn to scale where the lesson depends on the ratio; otherwise marked "not to scale".
- [ ] A recomputation by a second method matches (a sum, an angle, a position).
- [ ] Balances balance (equations, energy, counts).

## Hedging and localising
- [ ] Where the text says "may", "can", "often", the picture does not look certain (weaker case staged, or dashed/half
      intensity).
- [ ] Nothing is localised that the source does not localise (a whole-organ glow, not one spot, unless a part is named).
- [ ] Nothing is quantified that the source leaves unquantified.

## Marks and refusals
- [ ] Every simplification (scale, number, speed, exaggeration, stand-in shape, conventional colour) is marked on screen or
      in the text alternative.
- [ ] Each sentence that could not be pictured truthfully is listed as "not pictured" with the reason; no invented
      mechanism fills the gap.
- [ ] No false school simplification is drawn as if true.

## Presentation honesty
- [ ] No tool, model or voice name visible.
- [ ] A reviewer who knows the subject has seen the final picture (timings, exaggerations, conventions).
- [ ] The hand-over note lists what was not looked at.
